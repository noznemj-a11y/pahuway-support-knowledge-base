# Guest Complaint Escalation Guide

## Purpose

This guide helps you decide whether to fix a complaint yourself or hand it to someone with more authority. It also explains how to hand it over so the guest does not have to repeat their story.

## When to use this article

- A guest is unhappy and the other articles do not fully solve the problem.
- A guest asks to speak to a manager.
- You are not sure whether you are allowed to offer a solution.
- A guest mentions safety, discrimination, theft, or legal action.

## Steps

1. **Listen first.** Let the guest finish. Do not interrupt or defend the hotel.
2. **Write down the facts:** guest name, room number, what happened, when it happened, and what the guest wants.
3. **Apologize sincerely** for the guest's experience. You can be sorry without admitting fault.
4. **Decide the level.**
   - **Level 1 (you can resolve):** small problems such as a missing towel, a slow request, or a minor noise issue. You may offer a free drink voucher worth up to PHP 300 (fictional amount).
   - **Level 2 (supervisor):** repeated problems, a refund request outside policy, a room change that needs approval, or compensation above your limit.
   - **Level 3 (duty manager):** safety concerns, injuries, damaged belongings, accusations of discrimination or theft, threats of legal action, or media or online review threats.
5. **Act on your level.** If it is Level 1, fix it and record it. If it is Level 2 or 3, tell the guest you are passing it up and give a time when they will hear back (within 30 minutes for Level 2 and within 15 minutes for Level 3).
6. **Hand over clearly.** Send the supervisor or manager your written notes so the guest does not need to repeat anything.
7. **Log the case** in the guest notes with the time, the summary, who took over, and the outcome.
8. **Follow up** after the case is resolved using the [follow-up template](../templates/follow-up-after-resolved-complaint.md).

## What to say to the guest

> Thank you for telling me about this, [Name]. I am sorry this has happened. I have noted everything you told me, so you will not need to explain it again. I am going to pass this to [our duty manager / my supervisor], [Name], who can [decide on this / arrange this] for you. You will hear from us by [time]. Is [phone / room phone] the best way to reach you?

## When to escalate

Escalate immediately (Level 3) if:

- Anyone is hurt or feels unsafe. Call the security desk on 000-0000 (fake number) first.
- The guest reports theft, harassment, or discrimination.
- The guest mentions a lawyer, a regulator, or posting publicly.

Escalate to a supervisor (Level 2) if:

- The guest asks for more than you are allowed to give.
- You have tried once and the guest is still unhappy.
- You are unsure. Asking is always better than guessing.
