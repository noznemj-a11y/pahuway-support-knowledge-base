# Room Key Card Troubleshooting

## Purpose

This article explains what to do when a guest says their room key card does not work. It covers quick fixes and how to issue a new card without putting the guest's safety at risk.

## When to use this article

- A key card does not open the door (no light, or a red light).
- A key card stopped working in the middle of a stay.
- A guest lost their key card.
- A guest is locked out of their room.

## Steps

1. Stay calm and polite. A guest locked out may be tired or worried.
2. Ask for the room number and the guest's name. **Never** give out or reset a key card by phone or chat without confirming who is asking.
3. Ask the guest to try again slowly: insert the card fully or tap it flat against the reader and wait for the green light. Cards often fail because they are moved too fast.
4. If the light is red or nothing happens, the card may be demagnetized (for example, from being kept next to a phone). Suggest getting a new one.
5. If the guest is at the front desk, check their photo ID and match it with the booking. If they cannot show ID, ask them to give details only the booked guest would know (for example, the booking's email address and dates).
6. Check the reservation to confirm the room and dates are correct. Also check the check-out date. A card stops working after the booked check-out time.
7. Make a new card and cancel the old card in the key system. This makes sure a lost card cannot be used. Issue up to 2 replacement cards per guest, free of charge.
8. If the guest is still in the hallway and cannot come down, send security to open the door, but only after step 5 is completed.
9. If a new card also does not work, the door lock battery may be dead. Send maintenance and offer a temporary alternative such as waiting in the lobby lounge.
10. Log the case: room number, time, cause, and what was done.

## What to say to the guest

> I am sorry about that, [Name]. Let me get you back into your room right away. To keep your room safe, I need to check your ID and booking first. It will only take a moment. I will make you a new card now and cancel the old one, so no one else can use it.

## When to escalate

Escalate to a supervisor or security when:

- The guest cannot prove they are booked in that room.
- Someone other than the booked guest is asking for access.
- A card seems to have been used by someone else (the key system shows door entries the guest does not recognize). Treat this as a possible theft and follow the [guest complaint escalation guide](guest-complaint-escalation-guide.md).
- The door lock is broken and the guest needs another room.
