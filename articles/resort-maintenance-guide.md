# Resort Maintenance Guide

## Purpose

This article helps you handle a guest's report of a maintenance issue — in their room or in a common area of the resort — and guides you through logging it, giving the guest a clear expectation, and knowing when to escalate.

## When to use this article

- A guest reports something broken, leaking, not working, or unsafe in their room (air conditioning, plumbing, electrical, furniture, appliances).
- A guest reports a maintenance issue in a common area (pool, gym, elevator, hallway lighting).
- A guest asks when a previously reported issue will be fixed.

## Steps

1. Ask the guest what is wrong, where it is (room number or specific common area), and how it is affecting their stay.
2. Ask whether there is any safety risk (for example, exposed wiring, a strong gas smell, standing water near outlets, a door or window that will not lock). Treat any safety risk as urgent — see escalation below.
3. Log a maintenance ticket with the room/area, description, and urgency level (Urgent / Standard / Low).
4. Give the guest a realistic time estimate:
   - **Urgent** (safety risk, no AC in extreme heat, no running water): technician within 30 minutes.
   - **Standard** (AC not cooling well, minor leak, appliance not working): technician within 2–4 hours.
   - **Low** (cosmetic issues, a flickering bulb, loose fixture): next available maintenance slot, same day if possible.
5. If the issue affects the guest's ability to stay in the room (no AC, no water, safety risk) and cannot be fixed quickly, offer a room move to a comparable or better room at no extra charge, subject to availability.
6. For urgent or room-move situations, also consider a small goodwill gesture (for example, a dining credit), within what you are authorized to offer. See the [apology for an inconvenience template](../templates/apology-for-inconvenience.md) if a written apology is appropriate.
7. Notify the guest once the technician is on the way and again once the issue is resolved. Use the [maintenance issue notification template](../templates/maintenance-issue-notification.md) for written updates.
8. Follow up with the guest after the visit to confirm the issue is actually fixed, not just that someone visited.
9. Close the maintenance ticket with the outcome and any compensation given.

## What to say to the guest

> Hello [Name], thank you for letting us know. I'm sorry about the [issue] in [room/area]. I've logged this with our maintenance team and a technician should be with you within [time estimate]. I'll follow up with you once it's taken care of.

If a room move is needed:

> Since this may take a bit longer to resolve, I'd like to move you to another room so you're not without [AC / water / etc.] in the meantime. I can have that ready within [time]. Would that work for you?

## When to escalate

Escalate immediately to maintenance/security and a supervisor when:

- There is any safety risk (electrical, gas, structural, water near power sources).
- The same issue has been reported more than once for the same room.
- The guest requests compensation beyond a small goodwill gesture.
- A room move is needed but no comparable rooms are available.

Never promise a fix time you have not confirmed with maintenance. Never promise compensation beyond what you are authorized to give — escalate instead.
